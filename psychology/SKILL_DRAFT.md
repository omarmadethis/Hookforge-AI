# Consumer psychology for viral product reels

## Core rule
Every creative choice must name (1) the principle, (2) the observable feature that carries it, and (3) how you'd check it in the finished video. If you can't point to a frame, a text card or an audio beat, it's not applied.

## The principles, and what they look like on screen
| # | principle (source) | observable feature | check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Information gap: curiosity peaks with partial knowledge (Loewenstein 1994, Psychol Bull; Kang et al. 2009, Psychol Sci) | Hook text withholds the answer while a partial cue is visible: a cut-off word ("doesn't exi-"), "The idea…", "Vs..", a glimpse of the glow or box | Answer revealed after 2 s; product or outcome partly visible by 1.5 s |
| 2 | **Gap needs a cue.** With zero information there's no curiosity (same sources) | Covered reveal is a proven hook when the product's own cue shows at 0 s: its colour, texture or face around a tray or muffin tin, or a drape in the product's own fabric or colour pulled off a display. A fully hidden product, or a cue that reads as a different product, gives a gap with no stakes | Hook frame shows the product's cue; reveal by 3.0 s |
| 3 | Orienting response / surprise (Sokolov 1963; Itti & Baldi 2009, Vision Res) | An unexpected event in 0–2 s: a fall, flare-up, chaos, absurd image; high first-second motion | Motion in the first 1 s is in the top quartile, or a scripted event at ≤1 s |
| 4 | Contrast / reference dependence (Kahneman & Tversky 1979) | Bad "then" → "Vs.." → good "now"; cringe emoji → payoff emoji | ≥3 cards; bridge card on a cut; payoff in the last 40% |
| 5 | Open loop → closure (Zeigarnik 1927; narrative transportation, Green & Brock 2000) | The question opened by card 1 is closed by the last shot | The final card or shot answers the hook |
| 6 | Peak–end rule (Kahneman et al. 1993, Psychol Sci) | Last shot = the most rewarding image (static hero, product fills ≥60%) | Last-shot product share ≥0.6 |
| 7 | Mere touch / endowment (Peck & Shu 2009, JCR; Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1990) | POV, over-the-shoulder or high-angle hands holding, unboxing, using the product | ≥2 body shots are hands on the product |
| 8 | Self-referencing beats watching a presenter (Escalas 2007, JCR) | Viewer-as-actor framing; ≤1 face-to-camera demo shot | face_visible share ≤0.4 |
| 9 | High-arousal emotion drives sharing (Berger & Milkman 2012, JMR) | Payoff = awe, excitement or amusement (lit reveal, flame, stunt), and energy that fits the product world | Payoff shot is high-energy for hype products, sensory and calm for calm products |
| 10 | Gifting psychology (Sherry 1983, JCR; Flynn & Adams 2009, JESP) | Recipient or gift framing; recipient reaction payoff | Hook names a recipient or gift problem |
| 11 | Benign-violation humor (McGraw & Warren 2010, Psychol Sci) | Absurd but harmless; nobody really hurt | No injury framing; laugh emoji only on goofy products |
| 12 | Baby-schema "aww" (Sherman, Haidt & Coan 2009, Emotion; Nittono et al. 2012, PLoS ONE) | A pet or kid reaction as a garnish | Not the hook |
| 13 | Sensory/ASMR appeal for calm content (Barratt & Davis 2015, PeerJ) | Close-up tactile and sound beats with native ambient SFX; calm music | ≥2 sensory CUs; no hype edit |
| 14 | Mere exposure (Zajonc 1968, JPSP) | A proven song at a proven section; one consistent text style | Song in the proven list; house text preset |
| 15 | Social proof (Cialdini, *Influence*; Goldstein et al. 2008, JCR) | Others visibly wanting or reacting (neighbours, crowd) | Shown, not claimed |
| 16 | Reactance / forbidden framing (Brehm 1966; Worchel 1992) | "Don't show this to…" | Category-dependent: test before scaling |
| 17 | Identity callouts (Tajfel & Turner 1979) | "Calling all X…" | Weak as the sole hook device; embed it inside a gap or contrast hook |
| 18 | Scarcity / urgency (Worchel et al. 1975) | RUN, limited, deadline | Not a reach lever for organic content; use sparingly |
| 19 | Reciprocity + low-friction CTA (Regan 1971; platform engagement-bait policies) | One-word keyword reply in exchange for the link or offer | Use "SAY <WORD>" phrasing, not comment/tag/vote bait |

## Emoji grammar (on-screen text)
- 1 face emoji for the emotion + optional 1 world emoji, at the end of the last line. Never 3+.
- Then-card = cringe (😬 🤕 🤮). Bridge = ⏳. Payoff = 🤩 😻 ✨. Hype/humor = 😂 🤣. Wearable warning = 😳 ❄️.
- Avoid 👇 in CTAs, and 👀 or 🎁 as the hook emoji. Keep holiday symbols (🎄) for payoff cards and captions.

## Shot grammar defaults
- Hook: an orienting event or contrast "then" (wide, handheld), OR the product-on-person displayed centre-frame, OR a covered reveal with the product's cue visible (static eye-level medium shot, product fills 35–60% of the frame; or a static wide-shot staff unveil of a drape in the product's colour), counted 3-2-1 and revealed by 3.0 s.
- Body: hard cuts every ~1.4 s with a new angle each cut; mostly POV, over-the-shoulder or high-angle hands; reactions in medium or wide shots.
- End: a static hero of the working or worn product.
- Setting must match the product's world (a ski product on the slope, a cozy product at home, at night).

## Scoring a draft (0–1 each; sum and compare with your own winners)
gap_with_cue · orienting_event_≤1s · contrast_arc · closure · peak_end_hero · hands_on_share≥0.33 · face_share≤0.4 · arousal_fits_world · emoji_grammar_ok · cta_keyword_ok. Calibrate weights on your own posted results; views are noisy, so prefer retention and shares where available.

## Guardrails
No race or ethnicity casting choices, no slurs, no false scarcity, no fake retailer endorsements without a clear twist or disclosure, and no comment/tag/vote bait.
